# The method: Hexa Lean Discovery

Sources: the Notion page « 🤓 Lean Discovery » (Founder Starting Pack, https://app.notion.com/p/2a71cc0bd5bc80dfb297d1a95044c8c4), the Discovery tracking page (https://app.notion.com/p/41c1cc0bd5bc810f818feb1c9eabc5f0) and the first discovery sprint run with this harness (Nov 2026). Re-read the Notion pages when in doubt: they win over this file.

## What discovery is

- A learning process to understand a space, its value chain and its pain points.
- Talking with target personas, domain experts, VCs and competitors.
- The goal is conviction and unique insights. A kill is a valid result.

## The sprint

- **One month**, extendable once. Dates in `project.json` (`sprint`).
- **Weeks 1–2:** intense discovery, wide map.
- **Around day 10:** a **draft proposal** to agree with the partner on the focus.
- **End of month:** a complete **IDEA Framework** (`1-idea/idea-framework.md`): the go / no-go document with the partner.
- **Every week:** the Weekly reviews row before the Friday discovery meeting (WTF Discovery Board), and the WTF async weekly if the studio asks for it.

## Success

- A-players met: 8 per discovery on the board, at least 20% of calls (5 is the floor).
- Calls: 40 counts as 50% of progress on the board; ~10 a week at the start.
- Proof of problem (PV1–PV4) and of solution (SV1–SV4), design partners ready to pay, then founder search launched.
- A complete IDEA Framework and a clear conviction.

## A-player

Did the job themselves, recently and more than once, and is in the target: buys, decides or runs the work. Experts and investors don't count as A-players (they are opinions). Ticked after the call by whoever ran it, with one line in "Why A-player".

## The order

1. Start from a written hypothesis (`1-idea/current.md`).
2. Map the whole value chain, from the need to delivery to the end customer: vocabulary, who does what, the pains at each step, what AI changes and how mature it is, by vertical, geography and size (`3-knowledge/value-chain.md`).
3. Zoom in, by rounds of 2–3 hypotheses, until you know what to sell to whom in under two months.

## Organise

- 🗺️ **Have a plan:** list what you want to learn, in priority, and therefore who you need to talk to.
- 🔥 **Keep the pace:** LinkedIn outreach, intros, many conversations (~10 a week at the start).
- Who: target personas (the ones who have the pain and the ones who pay), experts, VCs, competitors. Large companies are usually better payers than startups.

## A good call

During the call:
1. Ice breaking.
2. Announce the call structure (and the recording).
3. Open questions about past facts; ask to **see** (a quote, a planning sheet, a contract, a tool).
4. Dig with layers of "why".
5. Referral: "who else should I talk to?"
6. Thanks.

Posture: be clear and easy to follow, lead the conversation, act as an expert who brings value and tips (what you learned from others). Listen for the "no"s that are expressed. If the person seems to waste their time, say it; if there is a problem, ask how serious it is for them.

After the call: take time to write what you take out of it, the implications and consequences, with the organisation's profile (to start segmenting) and the key learnings (skill `call`).

Control points: the person enjoyed the discussion; you have notes with the organisation profile, the key learnings and your reflection; you have take-aways.

## The Mom Test, in short

- Talk about their life, not your idea.
- Ask about specifics in the past ("the last time…"), not generics or opinions about the future.
- Never ask "would you pay for…". Look for what they already spend (time, money, workarounds).
- Commitment (time, data, intro, money) is the only real signal of interest.
