# Writing an outreach message

Updated in Nov 2026 from the Hexa discovery DMs that get answers and calls (pattern below, examples in [`linkedin.md`](linkedin.md)). The original rules come from two open-source cold-email skills (sources at the bottom), adapted to discovery: we ask questions, we sell nothing. They apply with [`linkedin.md`](linkedin.md) and [`email.md`](email.md) to every first message and follow-up the harness proposes. The owner sends by hand.

## The pattern that works

1. **"Hello {first name},"**, informal ("tu" in French).
2. **Credibility first**, the strongest you have:
   - an intro: « Je bosse avec {name} chez Hexa, il m'a conseillé de t'écrire. » / « J'ai déj hier avec {name}, il m'a conseillé de t'écrire. »;
   - otherwise, the Hexa company closest to their world (`{anchor}`): « Je suis chez Hexa, le startup studio derrière {company} ({what it does in 3 words}). » Add a true detail that concerns them when there is one (their company is a customer or partner of that company, for example).
3. **The mission in one sentence, in plain words:** « On veut lancer notre prochaine startup {domain}, sur {topic} ». No pitch, no solution, no jargon.
4. **A short personal hook, if you have one:** « (avec ton expérience chez {company}) ». It is the CRM `Personal line`: an intro, a relevant experience or a dated signal, always true and sourced.
5. **One ask with a precise slot:** « J'ai quelques questions pour toi. Ça te dirait d'en parler 20 min ce jeudi ? » or « Tu aurais 20 min aujourd'hui ou la semaine prochaine ? ». 15 or 20 minutes. A ";)" at the end works.

Length: 3 or 4 sentences. A LinkedIn connection note stays under 300 characters (count them).

## The rules

1. **Credibility opens the message** (an intro, or the closest Hexa company), then the mission. Never "I hope you're well", "I'm reaching out", "My name is".
2. **Talk like a peer.** A founder writing to someone who knows the field, not a salesperson. It must sound right read aloud.
3. **Every sentence earns its place.** Connection note: 300 characters max. Message: 3–4 sentences. Email: 60–90 words.
4. **One easy, dated ask.** "20 min this Thursday" beats "whenever suits you". With a busy executive: "Is this you, or rather your <team>?"
5. **Fit the anchor and the topic to the person.**
   - Anchor: the Hexa portfolio company closest to their sector, from the anchors table of `6-templates/linkedin.md` (filled at setup with the owner); otherwise "the studio behind Aircall and Spendesk".
   - Topic in their words, not ours.
   - Executive: 2–3 sentences. If they forward to their team, ask for a named intro.
6. **No AI tells.** No em dash. No "it's not X, it's Y". No question followed by its own answer. No "Quick question" opener. No jargon ("synergy", "leverage", "innovative solution"). No flattery ("great post"). Never two identical messages where only the first name changes: the anchor, the topic or the hook changes.
7. **A follow-up brings something.** "Just following up" gives no reason to answer. Add something new: what someone else in their field told us (unnamed unless allowed), or a sharper question. Readable without the first message. One follow-up only, 5 working days later.

## LinkedIn

- **Before the request (optional):** view the profile, comment on a recent post with a real comment.
- **Connection note:** the message above often fits in 300 characters. Otherwise, connect without a note and send the message after acceptance.
- **Limits (orders of magnitude from tool vendors):** 15–25 requests a day, about 100 a week. Below 25–30% acceptance, LinkedIn sees unwanted requests. Withdraw requests pending for 2–3 weeks.
- **The owner's profile:** everyone contacted looks at it. The headline must say what they do.
- **No automation tool:** they break LinkedIn's terms, and the budget is €0.

## Email

- **Subject:** short, plain, like a mail between colleagues. No urgency, no emoji, no fake "Re:".
- **Body:** the same pattern, plain text, at most one link.
- **Opt-out:** the "no thanks" line of the template stays in every email.

## Check before proposing a message

- Would the owner have written it? Compare with the examples in [`linkedin.md`](linkedin.md).
- Is the credibility the strongest available (intro first, else the closest Hexa company)?
- Is the hook true and sourced?
- One ask, with a precise slot?
- Length checked (characters counted for a note)? No AI tell?
- Right language: the person's (English for US contacts)?
