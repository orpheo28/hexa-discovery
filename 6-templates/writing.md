# Writing an outreach message

These rules come from two open-source cold-email skills (sources at the bottom), adapted to discovery: we ask for advice, we sell nothing. They apply with [`linkedin.md`](linkedin.md) and [`email.md`](email.md) to every first message and follow-up the harness proposes. The owner sends by hand.

## The 7 rules

1. **Start with the person, not with us.** The personal line opens the message. Never "I hope you're well", "I'm reaching out", "My name is". Test: if the message still works without the personal line, the line is useless. It must lead to the question.
2. **Talk like a peer.** A colleague who noticed something, not a salesperson. "You" more often than "I". It must sound right read aloud.
3. **Every sentence earns its place.** LinkedIn connection note: 300 characters max. Message after acceptance: 150–200 characters. Email: 60–90 words.
4. **One easy ask.** One question at the end, answerable in one line. 20 minutes is the normal ask, since the call is the goal. With a busy executive, a lighter ask works: "Is this you, or rather your <team>?"
5. **Fit the role.**
   - Executive: 2–3 sentences on the stake (cost, time, risk), no process detail. If they forward to someone on their team, good: in discovery that is often the right person. Ask for a named intro.
   - Operator (the person who does the work): concrete (last incident, tools, numbers).
   - Expert: what you read from them and the precise question.
6. **No AI tells.** No em dash. No "it's not X, it's Y". No question followed by its own answer ("The result? …"). No "Quick question" opener. No jargon ("synergy", "leverage", "innovative solution", "leader"). No flattery ("great post"). Never two identical messages where only the first name changes.
7. **A follow-up brings something.** "Just following up" gives no reason to answer. When there is something new, add it: what someone else in their field told us (unnamed unless allowed), or a sharper question. Readable without the first message. One follow-up only, 5 working days later.

## LinkedIn

- **Before the request (optional):** view the profile and comment on a recent post with a real comment.
- **Connection note:** only with a precise, true reason. Without one, send the request without a note, then the message after acceptance. (One agency study of 2025 quoted by the source: a note lowers acceptance a little, about 25% vs 28%, but raises replies, 8% vs 5%.)
- **Limits (orders of magnitude from tool vendors):** 15–25 requests a day, about 100 a week. Below 25–30% acceptance, LinkedIn sees unwanted requests: fewer, better-targeted requests. Withdraw requests pending for 2–3 weeks.
- **The owner's profile:** everyone contacted looks at it. The headline must say what they do; recent activity should show a few posts or comments.
- **No automation tool:** they break LinkedIn's terms, and the budget is €0.

## Email

- **Subject:** short, plain, like a mail between colleagues. No urgency, no emoji, no fake "Re:".
- **Body:** plain text, no image, at most one link.
- **Opt-out:** the "no thanks" line of the template stays in every email.

## Check before proposing a message

- Would the owner answer this message?
- Is the personal line true, sourced, and does it lead to the question?
- One ask only?
- Length checked (characters counted for LinkedIn)?
- No AI tell from rule 6?
- Right language: the person's (English for US contacts)?
